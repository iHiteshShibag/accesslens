## Strategic inference: what judges are most likely to reward

1. **Gemini performing the irreplaceable middle step.** A judge should be able to point at the image understanding and structured reasoning, not merely a chatbot wrapper.
2. **A working, fast, end-to-end prototype.** The event repeatedly prioritizes a working demo, and the analogous event weighted implementation and presentation heavily.
3. **Visible real-world usefulness.** A familiar problem, named user, and measurable before/after story beat speculative platform breadth.
4. **Evidence of judgment.** Confidence, constraints, safe fallbacks, and human verification show technical maturity.
5. **A memorable 90-second demo.** One input, one reveal, one action, one changing metric.
6. **Clear visual polish and honest data labels.** UX and presentation are explicitly listed in the overview.

These are inferences because Turbhe publishes no weights beyond its single Gemini criterion.

## Likely crowded project categories

Based on the prior AITD gallery, generic Gemini hackathon patterns, and the event’s beginner-friendly framing, expect:

- resume, internship, career, and interview copilots;
- study tutors, quiz generators, and PDF/lecture summarizers;
- personal-finance and receipt assistants;
- mental-health or symptom chatbots;
- generic “AI for government schemes” navigators;
- crop/plant image diagnosis;
- travel planners and local recommendation chatbots;
- broad multi-agent productivity dashboards;
- fake-news/certificate verification;
- chat-first accessibility assistants.

The common failure mode is breadth without a verifiable core workflow. A polished “AI operating system” may sound ambitious but can be hard to prove in a short live demo.

## Underserved opportunity

Small shops, clinics, cafés, colleges, and community venues often know accessibility matters but lack an affordable first step. A conventional audit requires site expertise, measurements, and time. A generic chatbot cannot see the entrance, and a score without evidence is untrustworthy.

The underserved wedge is **decision support before a professional audit**: turn one ordinary photo into evidence-linked questions and a prioritized list of practical fixes, while making uncertainty and human verification explicit.

This is narrow enough for one day, visually compelling, and naturally multimodal.

## Recommendation

### Primary: AccessLens

- **Target user:** a small-venue owner/manager or campus/community organizer who wants to improve access but does not know what to inspect first.
- **Pain point:** accessibility barriers are easy to overlook; professional audits are valuable but can feel costly or premature; generic checklists lack context.
- **Unique insight:** the best AI role is not to “certify accessibility.” It is to make invisible barriers discussable by linking every suggestion to visible evidence, confidence, affected users, and a next action.
- **Value proposition:** in under a minute, convert one photo into a reviewable accessibility action plan, then track how completed fixes change the score.
- **Measurable impact:** barriers surfaced, quick wins identified, fixes completed, score delta, and clearly labeled estimated monthly reach.

### Lower-risk backup: NoticeNav

Photograph a college, government, hospital, or workplace notice. Gemini extracts dates and requirements, rewrites it in plain Hindi/Marathi/English, and creates a checklist plus reminders. The demo is lower risk because OCR/summarization is reliable and the UI can reuse the same capture → structured output → action flow. It is less differentiated, so use it only if live visual-accessibility output proves unreliable.

## Smallest impressive demo

1. Dashboard establishes the problem and displays labeled synthetic baseline metrics.
2. User selects the bundled synthetic pharmacy entrance and confirms venue context.
3. Gemini analysis shows three meaningful stages.
4. Results overlay four numbered evidence regions on the photo.
5. Each finding shows severity, visible evidence, affected users, and confidence.
6. Plan ranks four fixes by priority, cost, effort, and score gain.
7. Completing the zero-cost fix moves the score from 42 to 50.
8. Hindi summary, impact formula, model-mode badge, and human verification complete the trust story.

Everything beyond this flow is optional. Authentication, multi-image 3D reconstruction, legal-standard certification, community maps, payments, and contractor marketplaces do not strengthen the judging-day proof enough to justify their risk.

## Fact/inference boundary in the pitch

Say:

- “The event formally requires meaningful Gemini API integration.”
- “AccessLens is an AI-assisted first pass, not a compliance certification.”
- “Our bundled scene and reach numbers are synthetic/illustrative.”
- “The live path supports real photos; the demo path is deterministic.”

Do not say:

- that Turbhe has published category weights;
- that Google is a listed sponsor when the public sponsor section is empty;
- that the IP clause has no conditions;
- that 8% of visitors are definitely disabled or helped;
- that a photo alone can prove legal compliance.
